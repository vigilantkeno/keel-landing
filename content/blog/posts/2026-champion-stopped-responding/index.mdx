The email from your champion stops mid-thread. No out-of-office, no
bounce, just quiet.

That silence isn't one thing.

## Three silences that look identical from your side

A champion going quiet reads the same from your inbox every time —
nothing arrives. What's actually happening underneath is usually one
of three things.

They changed roles internally, and the deal isn't dead, just
orphaned. They got let go, and the deal genuinely has no owner
anymore.

Or they're still in the seat, still employed, and just avoiding a
conversation they don't want to have with you. Each one needs a
completely different next move.

## If they changed roles

This is the best version, and it's more common than it feels in the
moment. People move teams, get promoted, get reassigned mid-quarter —
constantly, for reasons that have nothing to do with your deal.

Check the obvious things first: their email bounce message, the
company directory, LinkedIn.

If they moved internally, the deal's champion changed, not the deal
itself. Someone still owns the problem you were solving — you just
don't know their name yet.

## If they got fired or let go

This one is worse, and it's also more common than most reps want to
admit. A layoff, a reorg, a manager who didn't survive a quarter — and
the deal loses its only internal advocate at once.

There's no soft version of this: you're starting the internal
relationship over.

Whoever inherits the account has none of the context your champion
built with you across months of calls. You're not re-pitching a
deal — you're introducing yourself to a stranger who happens to have
a half-finished evaluation sitting on their desk.

## If they're still there and just went quiet

This is the one that stings, because it's rarely about you. Something
happened internally that your champion doesn't want to explain — they
lost an internal argument, got told no by their own boss, or simply
can't justify the deal anymore and don't know how to say that.

Champions go quiet on you the same way prospects do, for the same
reason: silence costs them nothing, and a real answer costs them an
uncomfortable conversation.

## The actual problem underneath all three

Here's what makes this harder than it should be: most reps only have
one source for what's happening inside the account — the champion.
When that channel goes dark, so does everything you knew about the
internal politics, the budget owner, the real timeline.

That's the deeper issue [customer ghosted](/blog/customer-ghosted)
covers from the buyer's side — the reasons for silence live in
details you never wrote down. With a champion, it's worse: you likely
never had a second person in the account who could confirm any of it
independently.

## What to do differently, starting now

Don't wait for the champion to resurface — go wide immediately.

Reach out to anyone else who sat in on a call, even once. Reference
something specific from that meeting, not a generic reintroduction.

If you truly have no second contact, that's the finding to act on
today, not after the silence confirms itself. A single-threaded deal
was always one departure away from this exact moment.
