Ask a sales rep why they don't want every call recorded and the answer
you'll usually get is a shrug, not a confession. There's nothing to
hide. There's just nowhere private to think.

## The default assumption gets it backwards

Most sales orgs treat privacy concerns as a yellow flag — something to
explain away, something a good rep shouldn't need.

That's exactly backwards. Reps who ask for privacy are usually the
ones being most honest about how they actually process a deal.

The ones who never ask aren't more transparent. They've just already
learned to keep the honest version somewhere the question won't reach.

## What it means to keep a sales conversation off the record

Every job has a register that isn't for the record. The version of a
thought you'd say to a peer, not put in a report.

Selling is unusually exposed already. The call gets scored. The
pipeline gets reviewed. The forecast gets questioned in front of
people.

Wanting one place that isn't any of that isn't evasion. It's the same
instinct that makes people process anything hard by talking it through
first, before anyone's grading the answer.

## The workaround is the feedback nobody reads as feedback

Raise a privacy concern directly and it tends to sound like an
objection. So most reps don't raise it — they just route around it.

The honest deal talk moves to a cell phone. The real read on a
stakeholder goes in a personal notes app instead of the CRM field
built for it.

None of that gets logged as "reps have privacy concerns." It gets
logged as nothing, because it's invisible by design — that's the whole
point of a workaround.

[We've written before about what that migration actually
costs](/blog/gong-anxiety): not less selling, just less of it
happening where anyone official can see.

## Honest sales conversations need an audience of one

Here's the distinction worth holding onto: privacy isn't the same as
withholding. Evasion hides information the business actually needs —
a stalled deal, a broken commitment.

Privacy protects the thinking that produces the honest version of that
same information in the first place.

A rep who processes a hard call alone before reporting on it isn't
skipping a step. They're doing the part of the job that only works
without someone watching it happen.

[The instinct to route around a monitored channel](/blog/shadow-it-sales-reps)
isn't reps going rogue. It's reps drawing a map of exactly where the
official tools stopped being safe to think out loud in.

## The concern was never the problem

A rep who wants privacy isn't telling you something's wrong with them.
They're telling you something true about where honest thinking can
survive.

Read it as a complaint and you'll keep losing the workaround. Read it
