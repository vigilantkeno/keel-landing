You close your laptop after a demo at 2:58 and you're in the hallway by
3:01, because the ops lead wanted to walk you out and used the thirty
seconds to say what she didn't want to say in front of her VP. That
conversation isn't on any calendar. By the time you're at your next call,
it's already competing with six other things for space in your head.

## Sales AI needs a meeting to exist before it can help you

Look at what "sales AI" means today and you'll find one assumption running
underneath all of it: something has to be scheduled first. A notetaker
needs a calendar invite to join. A call tool needs a dial-in to attach to.
The whole category was built around the meeting as the unit of work.

That's a narrow definition of when selling happens. The hallway aside. The
comment in the elevator on the way down. The two minutes before a demo
loads while everyone's making small talk. The stakeholder who catches you
in the parking lot because he didn't want to say it with his boss in the
room. None of it has an event ID. None of it exists as far as your stack
is concerned.

So when reps go looking for AI for unscheduled moments, most of what turns
up is still built around the calendar — a faster notetaker, a better
transcript. Useful for the meeting. Useless for everything around it.

## The in-between moment is often where the deal actually turns

Here's the part that should bother anyone building a sales stack: the
unscheduled moment usually carries more signal than the meeting it's
between, not less.

The scheduled call is where a deal gets presented — the deck, the demo,
the agenda everyone agreed to in advance. The in-between moment is where
someone drops their guard, because there's no agenda and no notetaker
watching. That's when a champion tells you what her boss actually thinks.
That's when the technical buyer admits the real blocker isn't your
product, it's a budget freeze nobody's said out loud yet.

Reps already know this instinctively. Ask any AE where they learn the
thing that changes their strategy, and it's rarely the meeting itself —
it's the walk to the elevator afterward, or the call a stakeholder makes
from his car once he's out of the building. The stack just never asked
those moments to matter.

## Where the in-between actually lives

Map a normal sales day and the unscheduled minutes outnumber the scheduled
ones. Five minutes before a demo, waiting for someone's screen share to
work. The hallway between two conference room doors. The elevator down
from a floor-nine meeting. A text that lands at 9:40pm from a prospect who
just finished reading your proposal alone. Someone catching you by the
coffee machine to ask a question they didn't want to ask in the room.

None of these fit a calendar invite. All of them are where a rep's
attention actually lives for a meaningful chunk of the day — and where the
software industry has nothing to offer, because nothing about the moment
looks like a "meeting" to a tool built to detect one.

Trade shows push this to the extreme. Three days, dozens of hallway
run-ins and booth conversations, none scheduled, all of it competing for
the same tired brain by day two. If a tool only works for the meetings on
your calendar, it's useless for the week where the most deal-relevant
conversations happen off it entirely.

## "Always available" is a different design than "attends more meetings"

Once you accept that the moments that matter aren't scheduled, the fix
isn't a better notetaker. A notetaker still needs something to attach to.
The fix is a tool that doesn't need an event to exist before it's useful —
one you can talk to from a hallway, a car, or a text thread at any hour,
because the moment it needs to catch doesn't wait for a calendar slot.

That's a different bar than most sales AI clears. It means voice and text
have to work the same way a phone call works: available now, no login, no
app to open first. It means the tool has no opinion about whether
what just happened was scheduled — it only cares whether it mattered to
the deal.

We've made a version of this case before about the moments
[call recording can't capture](/blog/what-call-recording-cant-capture) even
when a recorder is technically running. The in-between moment takes it one
step further: there's no recorder to begin with, because there was never a
call.

## What capture actually needs to look like

The bar for a tool built around unscheduled moments is speed, not
sophistication. A rep walking out of a hallway conversation has maybe
ninety seconds before the next thing pulls their attention. If the tool
needs a login, a project to file into, or a keyboard, the moment is gone
before the capture starts.

So it has to be a phone call or a text, not an app to open. Talk through
what the ops lead said on the walk out, in the time it takes to get back
to your desk, and it's saved — no tagging, no deciding which deal folder
it belongs in. That's the same instinct behind building a
[second brain for sales reps](/blog/second-brain-for-sales-rep): the
capture has to fit inside the gap, or the gap wins and the detail never
gets recorded at all.

## Why this only works if it's built for the rep, not the org

There's a reason unscheduled moments have been ignored by sales software
longer than they should have been: they're the moments a company has the
least visibility into, and most sales tools are built to increase that
visibility, not accept it. A hallway comment reported up to a dashboard
stops being the kind of thing anyone says honestly in a hallway.

An AI for in-between moments only earns access to those conversations if
it's [rep-first by design](/blog/rep-first-ai-sales) — working for the
person having the conversation, not surfacing it to anyone else. The
stakeholder who caught you at the coffee machine wasn't talking to your
company. She was talking to you.
