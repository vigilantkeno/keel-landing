Every deal starts with the same five questions.

Budget. Authority. Need. Timeline. Sometimes a sixth, if the framework has one.

Ask them once, get answers, move the deal to the next stage.

## What "process" usually means here

BANT, MEDDIC, CHAMP — pick a letter framework and the shape is the same. A checklist, run once, usually during discovery.

Pass it, and the deal is "qualified." The checklist doesn't get asked again.

| Framework | What it checks | When it's usually asked |
|---|---|---|
| BANT | Budget, authority, need, timeline | Once, at intake |
| MEDDIC | Metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria and process, pain, champion | Once, during qualification |
| CHAMP | Challenges, authority, money, prioritization | Once, during discovery |

[MEDDIC questions](/blog/meddic-questions) breaks down that specific list in detail. The gap here isn't which set of letters you pick.

## The gap every framework shares

None of them get asked twice.

A deal qualified in week one on "budget confirmed" can lose that budget in week six — a new fiscal year, a reorg, a VP who leaves. The framework already did its job. It doesn't come back to check.

[Lead qualification checklist](/blog/lead-qualification-checklist) names this directly. A checklist item can be true at intake and false two weeks later, and nothing in the process re-asks it.

## What actually needs to be a process

Not a gate. A recheck, run at the moment something changes — a stakeholder goes quiet, a champion changes roles, a date slips without explanation.

[B2B lead qualification](/blog/b2b-lead-qualification) makes the case that the signals worth trusting are behavioral, not stated. That means they show up mid-deal, not at intake — exactly where a one-time framework is structurally built to miss them.

The rep already notices when a "qualified" deal stops feeling qualified. No framework asks them to write that down.

## The budget that disappeared

Elena qualified the deal in March. Budget confirmed, economic buyer named, MEDDIC box checked.

In May, the VP who owned that budget took a new job. Nobody re-ran the checklist — there was no step in the process that said to.

Elena caught it anyway, from a message a mutual contact forwarded her. She'd already flagged that VP as a flight risk in her own notes back in April, for a completely different reason.
