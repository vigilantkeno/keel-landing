Marcus has known his buyer for four years before the deal actually
closes. Three of those years, there was no active opportunity — just a
relationship he kept warm on purpose.

That's not a sales cycle most AI sales tools were built to see.

## Relationship selling doesn't fit the "deal in progress" model

Most sales AI is built around an open opportunity: a stage in a
pipeline, a close date, a forecast category. The tool wakes up when a
deal opens and goes quiet when it doesn't.

Relationship selling runs on a longer clock. The important moments
happen outside any open deal — a call just to check in, a note after a
buyer changes jobs, a conversation that has nothing to do with a
current opportunity and everything to do with the next one.

A tool that only pays attention when there's a deal open misses most
of what relationship selling actually is.

## The real work happens between deals

Ask a relationship-driven rep what actually won their last deal, and
the honest answer rarely starts the day the opportunity opened.

It starts months or years earlier — a favor done with nothing asked in
return, a hard truth told when it would've been easier to stay quiet, a
relationship maintained through two job changes before this deal ever
closed.

None of that shows up as pipeline activity. All of it is the actual
sales motion.

## What gets lost without a place to put it

The problem isn't remembering the big things — the funding round, the
new title, the kid starting college. Reps remember those.

It's the smaller thread that goes missing: what a buyer said they were
worried about eighteen months ago, before the fix even existed. What
you promised to follow up on, once their situation changed.

The private read on where someone actually stands never made it into a
deal record, because there was no deal yet. That thread is what makes
relationship selling work, and it's exactly what falls out of a system
built around open opportunities.
