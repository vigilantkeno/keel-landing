Priya's second week on the job, her manager pulls up her call
recordings next to a rep who's closed deals here for three years.

Her talk-ratio looks bad next to his. It would look bad next to
anyone's, four weeks in.

## Most sales AI is tuned on a season of history that hasn't happened yet

Most sales tools are built around an established rep's baseline —
years of calls, a settled rhythm, a talk-ratio that already found its
groove.

A new rep doesn't have that yet. She has four weeks and a product
she's still learning to describe without notes.

Measured against a tenured rep's numbers, almost anything she does
reads as a gap. That's not a signal about her. It's a mismatch between
what the tool assumes and what week four actually looks like.

## The real problem: there's no baseline yet to build from

A rep with a year in the seat has an unspoken standard to check a new
call against — her own last quarter.

A new rep has no "last quarter."

Every call she takes is the first entry in the record, not a deviation
from one already there. Most tools weren't built for that starting
condition — they assume the baseline already exists somewhere and just
needs surfacing.

## Scoring her against the team average isn't ramp. It's a verdict.

Call scoring and team scorecards exist to catch an established rep
drifting from a norm. A new rep isn't drifting — she has nothing yet
to drift from.

Score her against the team average in week three, and the scorecard
reads like a verdict, not a coaching tool.

The gap it's measuring isn't a performance problem. It's a ramp
problem, wearing a performance problem's clothes.

## What ramp actually needs: a record that starts at zero, honestly

The useful version of this doesn't compare a new rep to anyone else.
It just holds what she's learning, in the order she learns it.

The objection that caught her flat-footed in week two. The answer she
fumbled and wants ready the next time it comes up. The call where a
buyer's tone shifted and she didn't yet know what that meant — three
calls later, she does, and the earlier note is what lets her connect
the two.

None of that needs a tenured rep's numbers next to it to be useful.

## This is the same gap [ADHD reps](/blog/ai-for-sales-rep-with-adhd) and [introverted reps](/blog/ai-for-sales-rep-introverts) already fall into

Different cause, same shape: a workflow built for one kind of rep
quietly fails everyone outside that shape.

The ADHD case is about when the capture happens — before the detail
evaporates. The introvert case is about where it happens — alone, not
performed for a room. The new-rep case is about what it's measured
against — nothing yet, and pretending otherwise is the mistake.
