"Struggling" gets used like it means everything is broken. Almost
never true.

Most struggling reps are actually fine at three stages of the deal
and consistently stuck at one.

## Four places a deal breaks

Discovery that never gets past surface-level answers. Prospects who
go quiet right after a strong first meeting.

Negotiations that drift instead of closing. Deals that "feel stalled"
without a clear reason why.

Each one is a different problem wearing the same word.

## Why "struggling" isn't a useful diagnosis

Told to a manager, "I'm struggling" gets answered with general
advice — more activity, better scripts, more confidence. None of that
fixes a specific broken stage.

A rep who's actually great at discovery and terrible at closing
doesn't need more discovery training. They need to know that's not
where the problem is.

Generic coaching treats every rep like they're bad at the same thing.

## Find your actual stage

Look at the last quarter's deals, not the ones that still sting the
most. Where do they consistently stop moving?

Same stage, over and over, across different prospects, different
industries, different deal sizes — that's not bad luck. That's the
actual location of the problem.

One stage, repeated, is a pattern. Different stages, occasionally, is
normal variance.

## Once you know the stage, the fix gets specific

"I struggle with sales" has no next action. "My deals stall after a
strong first call because I don't have a real reason to reach back
out" has an obvious one.

[New reps](/blog/ai-for-new-sales-rep) hit this constantly in the
first 90 days, because they haven't yet seen enough deals to know
their own pattern. Struggling reps at any tenure have the same fix:
find the pattern first, then practice the narrow thing.

## The habit that actually surfaces the pattern

The pattern is invisible without a record of what happened at each
stage, call by call — not a gut feeling about "not being good
