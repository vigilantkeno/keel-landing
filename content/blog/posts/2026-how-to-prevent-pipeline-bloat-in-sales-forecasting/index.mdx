A bloated pipeline doesn't look bloated. It looks like coverage.

3.5x looks healthy on a slide. Nobody asks how many of those deals are
actually still moving.

That question is the whole problem, and no forecasting model answers
it for you.

## What bloat actually is

Not too many deals. Too many deals that are already dead and haven't
been told so.

[A rep already knows, privately, which ones those
are](/blog/sales-pipeline-health). The pipeline doesn't know, because
nobody updated the field.

## Why nobody updates the field

Marking a deal closed-lost costs something immediate. The coverage
number drops. Someone might ask why.

Leaving it alone costs nothing today. [The bill comes later, when a
forecast built on that number turns out to be
wrong](/blog/pipeline-forecasting) — by which point the deal has been
sitting stale for a full quarter, not a week.

Every dead deal left in the pipeline is a small, rational decision.
The pipeline just accumulates all of them at once.

## Why smarter forecasting doesn't fix it

A better model still reads the same stage field. If the field says
"late-stage" for a deal that's actually gone, the model produces a
confident number built on a false input.

[Weighting, regression, an AI layer scoring the deal — none of it
touches the stage itself](/blog/pipeline-forecasting). The bloat moves
downstream unchanged. It just arrives dressed as math.

## The framework: force the decision, don't wait for it

Bloat only shrinks when silence stops being a safe default. Four
checks do most of the work.

- **Age thresholds per stage.** A deal sitting past a set number of
  days in one stage triggers a required reconfirmation — not a
  reminder email a rep can ignore.
- **Reason-coded reconfirmation.** "Still active" isn't enough. The
  rep names why: a specific next step, with a date. No date, no
  reconfirmation.
- **A default that isn't silence.** Untouched past the threshold
  should move a deal toward closed-lost automatically, not leave it
  parked until someone remembers to look.
- **Separate the read from the reminder.** [The clock-checking part is
  safe to automate](/blog/sales-pipeline-automation). Whether the deal
  is actually still alive stays a judgment call, made by the rep who
  was on the last one.

## What this looks like against a real pipeline review

A deal that clears reconfirmation with a real next step stays, and now
the forecast can trust it.

A deal that can't produce one gets marked honestly, before it ever
reaches [the meeting where the number gets
defended](/blog/sales-pipeline-review).

The coverage ratio might drop. That's the bloat leaving, not the
pipeline getting worse.
