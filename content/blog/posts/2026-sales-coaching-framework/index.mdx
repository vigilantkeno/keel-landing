Your manager's coaching template opens with "What's your goal here?"

You're not trying to find a goal. You're trying to figure out why the champion went quiet after the pricing call.

The framework doesn't have a box for that.

## Where the framework actually came from

GROW — Goal, Reality, Options, Will — comes from executive coaching. It was built in the 1980s to help a person clarify what they want and commit to a step toward it.

Sales teams adopted it wholesale. Same four questions, new context, no redesign.

It works fine for a rep who's lost and needs direction. It works badly for a rep who already knows exactly what's wrong with a deal and needs somewhere to put that read.

## The mismatch, one question at a time

"What's your goal?" A rep coaching on a stalled deal already has one: get the champion talking again. The question doesn't surface anything new.

"What's the reality?" This is the only question that touches the deal at all — and it gets one slot out of four.

"What are your options?" Generic options, generated on the spot, in a room with no notes from the actual call.

"What will you do?" A commitment made without the deal's specifics in front of anyone. It rarely survives contact with Tuesday.

Three of the four questions are about the rep. One is about the deal. [Deal coaching](/blog/deal-coaching) exists because that ratio is backwards — the deal is usually the part actually worth an hour.

## A framework built around the deal, not the rep

Four questions again, aimed somewhere else:

What changed. Not since last quarter — since the last call that touched this account.

What you already suspect. Before anyone else weighs in. [Effective sales coaching questions](/blog/effective-sales-coaching-questions) are the ones that test this honestly instead of accepting the first answer.

What's actually blocking the next step. Not a skill gap. A specific stall, named.

What you're trying next, and why. Tied to what's true about this deal, not a generic action item.

Same four-question shape GROW uses. Completely different four questions.

## Why the swap rarely happens on its own

Nobody sat down and chose GROW for sales coaching. It arrived because it's the framework every manager already learned somewhere else, and no one built a sales-specific one to replace it.

[Sales coaching software](/blog/sales-coaching-software) is where that gap usually gets filled — badly, if the software just digitizes the same four generic boxes instead of asking about the deal.

## The template that finally got rewritten

A sales manager at a twelve-person team retired her GROW template after eight months of one-word answers under "Reality."

She replaced it with one question: what's different about this deal since we last talked about it.

Her reps started writing paragraphs. The coaching conversation got shorter, because most of the diagnosis had already happened on the page.
