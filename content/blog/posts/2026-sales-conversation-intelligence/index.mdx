"Intelligence" is a strong word for counting.

Sales conversation intelligence platforms promise to understand a
call. Read the fine print on what they actually ship, and most of it
is pattern detection running on a transcript.

**That's a real capability.** It's also a narrower one than the name
suggests.

## What gets counted

Talk-to-listen ratio. Sentiment, scored word by word. Named
competitors, named objections, a running tally of both.

Every one of these is a proxy — a countable stand-in for something
that isn't directly countable. A rising objection count might mean a
deal is getting harder. It might mean the prospect finally trusts you
enough to push back.

The system can't tell which. It just counts the pushback.

## What understanding would actually require

A rep who's run forty of these calls doesn't need the ratio to know a
prospect went cold. They heard it in the pause before "let me think
about it."

That read is contextual, embodied, built from everything the rep
already knows about this specific account. It isn't reducible to a
score, because the thing being judged — is this hesitation real —
was never a countable quantity to begin with.

[AI call analysis](/blog/ai-call-analysis) makes a version of this
same argument: the useful output of a call isn't a number, it's a
same-day read from the person who was on it.

## Why the name persists anyway

"Conversation intelligence" sells better than "transcript pattern
counter."

The gap between the two isn't dishonest, exactly. It's aspirational —
a category naming itself after the job it wishes it did, not
necessarily the one it does.

[Call analytics tools](/blog/call-analytics-tools) breaks the market
into the three shapes that actually ship. None of the three is
understanding, in the sense the category name implies.

## Count what's countable, ask about the rest

Talk ratio and sentiment trend are real signals. Keep them.

Just don't mistake the count for the read. The word "intelligence"
was always promising the second thing — a promise the pattern-counting
underneath was never built to keep alone.
