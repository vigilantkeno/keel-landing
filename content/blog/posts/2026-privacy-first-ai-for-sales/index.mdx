Three vendors send Priya the same one-pager this quarter. Each says
"privacy-first" on page one, before pricing, before the demo link.

She can't tell the three apart from the copy alone.

## "Privacy-first" is a marketing word now, not a design description

Every AI sales tool claims it. Few define it.

Some mean the servers are encrypted. Some mean a vendor will delete
your data if you ask. A handful mean actual customer conversations
were never captured to begin with — a different claim, buried under
the same three words.

The label tells a buyer nothing. The design underneath it tells a
buyer everything.

## The one question that actually tests it

Ask a single thing: does the tool capture audio or a transcript of a
customer conversation, in any form, ever?

Not how the data is secured. Not what the retention policy says.
Whether anything gets captured at all.

A vendor that captures nothing has a one-sentence answer. A vendor
that captures something has a policy to describe — and a policy is
already a concession that the capture happened.

## Three follow-ups that surface the real design

If the answer is "yes, but," three follow-ups separate real
privacy-first design from a privacy-first label sitting on a
recording product.

Where does the recording live once it exists, and who inside the
vendor's company can open it? How long is it kept, and can the rep
who generated it actually delete it themselves? If the customer asked
whether the call was recorded, would the honest answer be yes?

A tool that never captures anything skips all three questions. There
is nothing left to ask about.

## What "privacy-first" doesn't mean

It doesn't mean compliant.

[Regulated buyers](/blog/sales-ai-for-regulated-industries) ask about
capture before they ask about encryption, for exactly this reason. A
tool can protect a recording carefully and still be the reason a deal
stalls in security review — the recording itself is the exposure, not
how well it's guarded afterward.

It doesn't mean the rep gave anything up, either.
[Reps who've watched a recording tool get repackaged as a privacy
feature](/blog/sales-rep-privacy-concerns) already know the
difference between a tool that protects their data and one that just
stopped bragging about collecting it.

## Where Sara fits

Sara doesn't record, transcribe, or store customer conversations —
not encrypted, not access-controlled, not deletable on request.
Never captured is a different design than captured-and-protected, and
it's the one that actually answers the first question in one
sentence.

That's a structural fact about how she's built, not a compliance
certification. Verify it with your own security team the same way
you'd verify any vendor's claim, ours included.
