Two demos, same Tuesday. Same five qualifying questions, asked the
same way.

Both prospects answered budget, authority, timeline, and pain without
blinking. On paper, identical leads.

One closed in six weeks. The other went quiet after the second call
and never came back.

## The list tests knowledge, not conviction

Every lead qualification list runs some version of the same five
questions. Budget, authority, need, timeline, competition.

They're good questions. They're just testing the wrong thing.

A qualifying question checks whether an answer exists. It doesn't
check who the answer belongs to.

## What actually separated the two leads

The prospect who closed answered from memory, in one breath, using her
own words for the pain.

The one who didn't paused on the timeline question. "Let me check with
our ops lead and get back to you." He did — three days later, with a
date that had clearly come from someone else.

Same five boxes checked. Different owner behind each answer.

## The follow-up question the list skips

Every standard question has an invisible second half: *how do you
know?*

Asked out loud, it's rude. Left silent, it's the actual signal.

| What the checklist asks | What the answer's speed tells you |
|---|---|
| "What's your budget?" | Instant, specific number: they've priced this internally already |
| "Who else needs to sign off?" | A pause, then a name they clearly haven't looped in yet |
| "What's your timeline?" | "This quarter" said flat vs. "let me check the roadmap" |
| "What's driving this now?" | A story with a date attached vs. a generic category |

None of these show up as a field in a CRM. They show up as a rhythm —
[the kind of signal a form can't fake](/blog/b2b-lead-qualification).

## Why the delayed answer is the useful one

A prospect who has to go check isn't lying. They're telling you,
accurately, that they don't yet own the problem.

That's not disqualifying on its own. Plenty of real deals start with a
champion who has to go get a number.

It's disqualifying when the *pattern* repeats across every question —
when budget, timeline, and authority all come back "let me check."
At that point you're not qualifying a lead. You're qualifying someone
else's willingness to ask their own boss the same five questions you
just asked.
