The call ends. You have two options, and they look almost the same
from the outside.

Option one: open the CRM, fill in what was discussed, move to the
next thing.

Option two: sit with it for ninety seconds and ask what actually
happened.

Most reps only ever do the first one — and call it reflection.

## The recap everyone calls reflection

A recap is a record of the call. What was on the agenda, what
questions came up, what the next step is supposed to be.

It's useful. It's also not reflection — it's transcription with
extra steps, just done from memory instead of a tape.

A recap answers "what happened." It never gets around to asking what
you noticed while it was happening.

## Recap vs. reflection

| | Recap | Reflection |
|---|---|---|
| Answers | What was discussed | What changed |
| Written for | The CRM, your manager | Yourself, next time |
| Source | Memory of the agenda | The read you had in the room |
| Decays | Slowly — facts hold | Fast — the felt sense fades first |
| Question it asks | "What happened?" | "What do I do differently?" |

The left column is a log. The right column is the only one that
changes how you sell the next call.

## Why the recap wins by default

Logging a call is a habit most sales orgs already train hard. Stage,
next step, done — the muscle memory is real.

Nothing trains the second habit. Nobody hands you a form for "what
felt off" the way they hand you one for "deal stage."

So the recap happens because it's asked for, and the reflection
doesn't, because nobody asked.

## What an actual reflection sounds like

Three questions, in order, before you open anything else.

**What changed.** Not what was discussed — what's different now than
before the call started.

**What's still sitting with you.** One line, one pause, one thing
they said that you keep coming back to.

**What you'd do differently next time.** Even one sentence. Especially
if it stings a little.

That's the whole method. Answering it out loud takes less time than
logging the call did.

## Timing is the whole trick

[We've written before](/blog/drive-home-from-sales-meeting) about
how sharp the read on a meeting is in the first few minutes after it
ends, and how fast that sharpness fades.

The same window applies to a phone call, a video call, any call.

Do the reflection at your desk an hour later, and you're not
reflecting on the call anymore. You're reflecting on a summary of a
summary.
