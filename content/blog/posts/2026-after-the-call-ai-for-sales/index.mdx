You hang up. Before the call even finishes ending, your attention is
already on the next thing — the Slack notification, the calendar
invite, whatever's next.

Somewhere in the sixty seconds you didn't take, the actual read on
that call went with it.

## AI for sales assumes there's a transcript

Ask what "AI for sales" means and most answers describe the same
thing: something that sits in on the call, produces a transcript,
and summarizes it afterward.

That assumes something was invited to sit in. A lot of sales calls
never have one.

Phone calls. A one-on-one the customer would rather keep informal.
[Conversations that happen where no meeting tool can follow](/blog/what-call-recording-cant-capture)
at all.

For those calls, there's no transcript to process. The tool built for
"after the call" has nothing to work from — so it just doesn't run.

## What actually fades in the first sixty seconds

The hesitation before "we're fine on budget." The name mentioned
once and never again. The tone that said more than the words did.

None of that survives in a transcript even when one exists — tone
and hesitation don't transcribe. They live in what you noticed, not
in what was said.

And what you noticed decays fast. Sharp for a minute, blurry within
the hour, gone by the time the next call starts.

## What AI for after the call should actually do

Not wait for a transcript. Ask you.

The version worth capturing is the one only you have: what changed,
what you noticed, what you'd do differently. Said out loud, right
after, before the next thing pulls your attention away.

[That's the actual work](/blog/deal-reflection-framework) — not
processing what got recorded, but catching what you noticed before
it's gone.
