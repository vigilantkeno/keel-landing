One meeting. One dashboard. Two different questions, asked as if
they're the same one.

"What's going to close this quarter" is a forecasting question. "Is
this deal actually healthy" is a pipeline management question.

Most sales orgs answer both from the same stage field, in the same
review. That's the whole problem.

## What each question is actually asking

Pipeline management is a diagnosis. It looks at one deal and asks
whether it's real — moving, on a true stage, with a next step that
isn't "circle back."

[Sales forecasting](/blog/sales-forecasting-techniques) is a
prediction. It takes every deal's diagnosis, weights it, and rolls it
into a single number leadership can plan against.

One is about a deal. The other is about a quarter. Conflate them and
neither question gets a clean answer.

## The dashboard that answers neither well

A weighted pipeline report looks like it's doing both jobs at once.

It isn't. [Forecasting math](/blog/pipeline-forecasting) can only be
as honest as the stage data feeding it — and stage data is a health
question that never actually got asked.

So the forecast comes out wrong, and the review spends its time
arguing about the number. Nobody goes back to check whether the deals
underneath it were ever healthy.

## Why the meeting always drifts toward the number

A number is easier to argue about than a deal.

["Sales pipeline health"](/blog/sales-pipeline-health) is slower work.
It means asking, deal by deal, whether the stage is true and the
timeline is real — not adjusting a rollup until it looks plausible.

Once the room is looking at a forecast number, that harder
deal-by-deal work almost never happens. The reflection is the work;
scoring confidence in a meeting is not.

## What happens when the two get separated

Diagnose deal health first, on its own terms, before any number gets
rolled up.

[A forecast broken out by rep](/blog/forecast-by-rep) can flag whose
number looks inconsistent with the deals behind it — but only once
health and forecast are two separate checks instead of one blended
guess.

Do the health check first and the forecast that follows it needs
almost no correcting. It was built on deals that were already telling
the truth.
