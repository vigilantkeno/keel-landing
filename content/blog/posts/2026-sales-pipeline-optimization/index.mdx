Every sales pipeline optimization guide runs the same six-item
checklist.

Stage-gate criteria, lead scoring, CRM field automation, enablement
content, forecast model tuning, and a cleanup cadence for stale deals.
Execute all six well and the dashboard looks sharper by Monday.

## What the checklist actually optimizes

Look at what each tactic actually touches.

Stage-gate criteria standardize when a deal moves columns. Lead
scoring ranks what comes in. CRM automation keeps fields populated
without a rep typing them by hand.

Enablement content gives reps something consistent to say. Forecast
tuning adjusts the model's weighting. A cleanup cadence flags deals
that haven't moved in 30 days.

Every one of these fixes the record. None of them fixes what the
record says.

## Tactic vs. what it actually changes

| Tactic | What it fixes | What it leaves untouched |
|---|---|---|
| Stage-gate criteria | Consistent movement between stages | Whether the rep believes the deal belongs there |
| Lead scoring | Which leads get worked first | Whether a scored lead is still alive |
| CRM field automation | Data completeness | Whether the data reflects reality |
| Enablement content | Message consistency | Whether the message fits this specific deal |
| Forecast model tuning | Weighting accuracy on average | Whether any single input was honest |
| Stale-deal cleanup cadence | Visibility into aging deals | Whether a rep flags a dead deal before the cadence does |

## The deal the checklist never asks about

Every rep is carrying at least one deal they privately know is dead.

Not stalled — dead. The buyer went quiet after a budget freeze.

The champion took another job. The timeline slipped a quarter with no
new date attached.

None of the six tactics above ask the rep to say so out loud.
Stage-gate criteria only fire when a deal moves; a dead one just sits.
Lead scoring never re-scores after the first pass.

[Sales pipeline health](/blog/sales-pipeline-health) is where that gap
actually shows up — not on any one tactic's dashboard, but in the
distance between total pipeline and the pipeline a rep would defend
under real questioning.

## What actually moves the number

Here's the intervention missing from the checklist: ask a rep,
privately, before the review, which of their own deals they'd
downgrade if nobody were watching.

Not in the CRM. Not in a shared doc.

Somewhere with no audience.

[Best practices for pipeline review meetings](/blog/best-practices-for-pipeline-review-meetings)
covers why that has to happen before the meeting, not during it. A rep
asked to admit doubt in front of a manager gives the safe answer,
every time.

Run that question first — then apply the six tactics to what's left.

An optimized pipeline that's already honest is a real improvement. An
optimized pipeline that isn't just makes the wrong number faster to
read.

## The dashboard was never the deal

A pipeline optimization checklist can be executed perfectly and still
report a number built on deals nobody in the room actually believes.

The checklist isn't wrong. It's answering a different question than
the one that decides whether you hit quota.
