MEDDPICC has a field for economic buyer. It does not have a field for
"I've never actually spoken to them."

That gap is where most deal assessments quietly stop being honest.

## The checklist that's supposed to keep you honest

MEDDPICC exists because gut-feel forecasting is unreliable. Name the
economic buyer. Name the metrics. Name the decision process.

On paper, filling it in should force honesty. You can't mark a field
true without knowing whether it's true.

In practice, the field lives in the CRM. A manager reads the CRM. So
the same incentive that distorts a pipeline meeting — say confidence,
not doubt — follows the framework straight into the form.

## Where the honesty actually leaks out

It rarely leaks out on the easy fields. Metrics and timeline are hard
to fake convincingly.

It leaks out on the fields that reward a generous interpretation.
"Economic buyer identified" gets marked yes because you have a name and
a calendar invite — not because that person has ever confirmed they can
sign.

"Champion" gets marked yes for someone who's been friendly on three
calls. Friendly isn't the same as willing to spend political capital
getting you a signature.

Nobody's lying, exactly. They're rounding up, the same way you'd round
up describing a deal out loud to your VP. [The reflection is the work](/blog/deal-reflection-framework) —
the meeting is the performance.

## The four questions the form doesn't ask

Before you touch the field, ask these privately. Nothing here gets
recorded anywhere.

Have you personally heard the buyer say something close to "yes, we're
doing this" — not inferred it, actually heard it?

If the deal died tomorrow, what's the real reason, in one sentence, not
the reason you'd give your manager?

Who in this deal have you never once spoken to directly?

What field are you about to mark "yes" that's actually closer to
"probably"?

That last one is the tell. It's the field you'll defend hardest if
someone asks a follow-up question — which is usually a sign you already
know the answer is shakier than the checkbox suggests.

## Why the order matters

Run the honest version after the CRM field is already filled in and
you're not assessing the deal. You're defending a decision you already
made.

Run it first, and the field becomes a record of something you already
know — not a guess you're hoping holds up. [Deal review without a manager](/blog/deal-review-without-manager) covers
the habit of doing this alone, before anyone else is in the room to
hear the answer. This is the narrower version: the specific four
questions worth running against MEDDPICC's blind spots, every time.

The gap between those two orders is small on any single deal. Across a
quarter of deals, it's the difference between a forecast built on what's
true and one built on what survived scrutiny.
