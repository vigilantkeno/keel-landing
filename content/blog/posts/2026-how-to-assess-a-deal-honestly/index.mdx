MEDDPICC filled out after the call reads like diligence.

It usually isn't.

## The framework was built for the wrong moment

MEDDPICC is a snapshot. Its honesty depends entirely on when you take it.

Fill it in mid-call, before you know how things landed, and every field
is a genuine guess.

Fill it in an hour later, once you already know whether you feel good
about the deal, and something changes. Every ambiguous field resolves
in the direction of the mood you walked out with.

## Confirmation bias with a nine-letter name

Take the Champion field. It asks whether this person actually has
influence, not whether they were friendly on the call.

Mark it "confirmed" after a call that went well. Mark the same evidence
"unconfirmed" after a call that didn't.

Nothing about the champion's actual influence changed in the forty-five
minutes between those two calls.

Your read on the call did.

The form isn't lying to you. The order you're filling it in is.

## What honest actually requires

Two changes fix most of this, and neither touches the framework itself.

First: write down what happened before you write down what you think it
means. Two separate passes, not one.

The observation is what was said, in whose words, in what order. The
verdict is your interpretation of it. Mixed together, the verdict edits
the observation before either of you notices.

Second: start with the field you trust least, not the one that flatters
you.

Most reps open MEDDPICC at Metrics — the easy, confidence-building
field — and end at Champion or Decision Process, the ones actually in
doubt.

By the time you reach the shaky field, you've already built momentum
toward a verdict. Start there instead.

Let the shakiest field set the tone for the rest of the form. Not the
other way around.

## Run it before, not instead of, the real review

[The deal reflection framework](/blog/deal-reflection-framework) covers
the full set of questions worth running against an open deal. This is
narrower: the specific habit of writing the observation before the
verdict, every time, before the story has a chance to set.

It also has to happen before the version you [run without a manager in
the room](/blog/deal-review-without-manager) — that one still needs an
honest input to start from, or it just launders the same story twice.

## The gap that actually needs filling

The habit only works close to the call, while the observation is still
separable from the story you're about to tell about it.

A week later, they're the same thing. You've forgotten which parts you
watched happen and which parts you decided afterward.
