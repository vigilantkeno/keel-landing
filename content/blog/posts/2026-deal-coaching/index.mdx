You hang up from a call where the champion said "we're aligned
internally" in a tone that didn't quite match the words.

Something's off. You can't name it yet — but the read is right there,
sharp and specific, gone by Thursday.

Thursday is when deal coaching happens. The 1:1 is already on the
calendar. This call isn't on the agenda for four more days.

## What "deal coaching" usually means

A scheduled session. A manager. A handful of deals reviewed in one
sitting.

That format works fine for the deals that haven't moved. It works
badly for the one that just did — an hour ago, in a call nobody's
reviewed yet.

## The gap between the moment and the session

By Thursday, the tone you heard has flattened into a bullet point.
"Champion aligned, moving to legal." That's the version that survives
four days of other calls stacking on top of it.

[The reflection is the work](/blog/deal-reflection-framework) — but
only if it happens close enough to the moment to still be honest. A
session built around a calendar instead of a call misses that window
by design, not by accident.

## Why the format doesn't move

Deal coaching got built around the manager's calendar, because that's
whose time was being scheduled. Not the rep's read. Not the moment the
read was sharpest.

That's a structural choice, not a law of nature. [Sales rep
autonomy](/blog/sales-rep-autonomy) covers the broader version of the
same argument — coaching that assumes someone else has to be in the
room before it counts.

## Deal coaching questions that work the same day

Four questions do more the same afternoon than a scheduled review does
four days later:

- What did you hear that didn't match what was said?
- Where did your certainty change mid-call?
- What would you honestly bet the deal's status is right now?
- What's the one thing you'd ask before the next call?

None of them need a manager in the room. They need to happen before
the read goes flat.
