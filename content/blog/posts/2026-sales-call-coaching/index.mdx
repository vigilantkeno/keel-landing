Search "sales call coaching" and most of what comes back teaches the
same thing to everyone.

Objection handling. Tonality. How to open a call, how to close one.

**That's a real skill.** It's also generic — the same lesson applies
whether the call was with a school district or a hospital system.

## What the skill layer actually covers

[Sales coaching software](/blog/sales-coaching-software) mostly
optimizes here. A rubric scores confidence, pacing, filler words.

Learn it once. Apply it to every call after.

That's genuinely useful. It's also not the part that decides whether
this specific deal moves.

## The layer skill coaching skips

A rep who nails the rubric can still misread whether last Tuesday's
silence meant real interest or a buyer who'd already moved on.

That read isn't technique. It's context — this account, this buyer,
this particular week.

[Deal coaching](/blog/deal-coaching) is the term for feedback aimed at
that layer instead. Most coaching software never gets there, because a
rubric built to score everyone the same way can't hold context specific
to one deal.

## Why timing decides which one you actually get

Skill coaching survives being delayed. A pitch technique taught next
week is still the same technique next week.

Deal coaching doesn't survive the wait. **The read on Tuesday's silence
has to happen close to Tuesday**, or it gets replaced by whatever
version feels true by Friday.

[How to reflect on a sales call](/blog/how-to-reflect-on-a-sales-call)
covers the mechanics of catching that read before it's gone.

## What sales call coaching actually needs

Three layers, not one:

- **The skill** — technique that transfers across every call you'll
  ever take.
- **The account** — what's specific to this buyer, this deal, this
  moment.
- **The timing** — close enough to the call that the read is still
  honest, not reconstructed.

Most coaching software ships the first layer and calls the job done.
