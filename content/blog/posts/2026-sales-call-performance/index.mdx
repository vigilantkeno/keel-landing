A call can score well and still lose the deal.

That gap is the whole problem with how "sales call performance" gets
measured.

## What gets counted

Talk-to-listen ratio. Sentiment, scored word by word. Pacing, filler
words, a tally of questions asked.

Every one of those is real, and every one is computable straight from
a transcript with zero human judgment involved. That's exactly why
they're the default — not because they predict what actually matters.

[Sales call transcript analysis
metrics](/blog/sales-call-transcript-analysis-metrics) breaks down all
four in detail. None of the four measure the thing a rep actually
needs to know.

## Why a good score can hide a bad call

A rep can land a clean 40/60 talk ratio and a warm sentiment trend
while dodging the exact question that would have surfaced a real
objection.

**The transcript looks great.** The deal didn't move an inch.

The reverse happens just as often — a messy ratio, an anxious-reading
sentiment score, and a deal that closes because one honest answer
landed. [Sales conversation
intelligence](/blog/sales-conversation-intelligence) is built to
count patterns like these. Counting was never the same operation as
understanding what happened.

## The one metric that actually counts

Performance has a simpler test: is the deal closer to done after the
call than before it?

That's not a transcript question. It requires the rep's own read —
did the buyer's hesitation shrink or grow, did a real objection surface
or stay hidden, would you bet differently on this deal now than an
hour ago.

[AI call analysis](/blog/ai-call-analysis) runs the same argument
further: the honest version of that read has a shelf life of about
twenty minutes before it gets smoothed into a story.

## What to actually track after a call

Skip the rubric. Ask four questions immediately, while the read is
still sharp:

- **Did the deal move?** Closer to done, or further away.
- **What surprised you?** Anything the buyer said that you didn't see
  coming.
- **What did you dodge?** The question you answered around instead of
  answering.
- **Would you bet differently now?** Compared to before the call
  started.

None of those four show up in a scored transcript.
