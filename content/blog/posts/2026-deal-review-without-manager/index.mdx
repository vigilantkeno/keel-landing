Every pipeline meeting has an audience. The deal review that actually
tells you something true has none.

That's not a coincidence. It's the whole reason the second one rarely
happens.

## The pipeline meeting rewards the wrong thing

A manager asks how a deal is tracking. The answer that gets rewarded
is confidence, not accuracy.

Say it's on track, name the next step, move to the next row. Nobody in
that room benefits from "I'm not sure this closes."

So the forecast gets built on the version of the deal that survives
scrutiny — not the version that's actually true.

## What a deal review without a manager is actually for

Take the manager out of the room and the incentive flips. There's no
one to reassure.

The only question left is whether you're right, not whether you sound
confident. That's a completely different exercise from the one most
reps are used to.

It has to happen before the manager review, though — not instead of
it. By the time you're presenting, you've usually already committed to
a story.

## Before the deal review, what to actually think about

Run three questions privately, right after the call that mattered:

Who has actually said yes, out loud — and who's just stayed quiet
while you assumed agreement?

What would have to be true for this to close on the date you've
already forecasted?

What are you hoping doesn't come up again, the next time someone asks
a hard question?

That third one is usually the most honest. It's also the one most
reps never write down anywhere.

## Why this is a deal health check, not a status update

A status update describes stage and next step. A real health check asks
whether the deal deserves the stage it's sitting in.

Those aren't the same question, and a pipeline review without a
manager has room to ask the second one — because there's no forecast
number riding on the answer yet.

[The deal reflection framework](/blog/deal-reflection-framework) goes
deeper into the full set of questions worth running. This is the
narrower version: the specific habit of doing it alone, before anyone
else is in the room to hear the answer.
