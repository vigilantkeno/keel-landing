Every talk-to-listen ratio tool produces the same number in the end.

Rep-talk divided by total-talk, rendered as a percentage on a dashboard.

## What's identical across every tool

The math never changes. Count the rep's words, count the total words, divide.

Vendors dress it up with sentiment overlays and question-count add-ons.
Underneath, it's the same fraction every tool on the market computes the
same way.

If the ratio itself were the differentiator, none of these tools would
need a sales team. It isn't, so they do.

## What actually differs

Four things separate a useful ratio tool from a decorative one — and
none of them show up in a demo.

- **Live or after the fact.** Some score the call while it's happening;
  most score the recording afterward, on whatever cadence the dashboard
  refreshes.
- **Whose baseline counts as normal.** A 45% ratio against a company-wide
  average and a 45% ratio against your own last twenty calls are
  different numbers wearing the same label.
- **What counts as listening.** Dead air reads as attentive silence in
  some tools and as a stall in others — same three seconds, opposite
  score.
- **Where the number goes next.** A ratio that reaches you before your
  next call is a different product than one that reaches you in Friday's
  digest.

Skim a pricing page and all four look identical. They aren't.

## Why the differences still don't reach the real question

Fix all four and the tool still can't tell you what actually matters:
did the deal move.

[Sales call performance](/blog/sales-call-performance) makes the
broader case — a clean ratio and a stalled deal show up on the same
transcript constantly. [Inside sales call metrics](/blog/inside-sales-call-metrics)
sorts talk ratio into the "conversation" family of metrics, next to
sentiment and question count. All three measure how the call was
conducted.

None of them measure whether it worked.

[Gong scorecards](/blog/gong-scorecards) turned that gap into a
standing rep complaint: the ratio moves every week, and the pipeline
underneath it barely does. A better-built talk-ratio tool narrows the
gap between the score and reality. It doesn't close it.

## What to actually check before you buy one

Skip the feature comparison. Ask four questions instead.

Does it normalize to your own baseline, not a generic benchmark built
from someone else's calls? Does its silence handling match how your
reps actually sell — pausing to let an answer land isn't the same as
going quiet because they're avoiding the hard question. Does the score
reach you before the next call, not after a week of them stack up. And
when the ratio and the outcome disagree, which one does the tool trust?

Most vendors can't answer that last one. The tool was never built to
notice the disagreement.
