Search "call intelligence solutions" and the demos start to blur
together.

A waveform. A transcript. A score climbing across four categories
nobody asked about.

Underneath the sameness, the products aren't the same. Four questions
separate what looks identical on the sales call.

## 1. Who sees the output first?

Ask before the demo ends: whose inbox does the score land in.

Some tools are built to answer upward first — one read across a whole
team, a rep's calls folded into someone else's report. Others hand the
read back to the rep who made the call, same day, before anyone else
sees it.

That's not a feature toggle. It's the design decision the rest of the
product follows.

## 2. How fast does the read reach you?

[AI call analysis](/blog/ai-call-analysis) already made this case: the
honest read on a call has a shelf life of about twenty minutes. A
solution that surfaces its output in a weekly report missed the window
before the report ever generated.

Ask what happens between the call ending and the insight showing up.

If the answer involves a batch job or a Friday summary, that's the
actual product — not the demo.

## 3. What happens to the raw material afterward?

Most call intelligence software keeps a permanent transcript archive,
indexed and searchable long after the deal closes or dies.

That's a retention decision as much as a feature. A transcript archive
isn't just a convenience — it's an artifact that outlives the
conversation it came from, sitting on a server somebody eventually has
to account for.

Ask who can pull it up six months from now, and why they'd want to.

## 4. Does it answer a question, or just score one?

A talk-ratio number, a sentiment score, a competitor-mention count —
all of that measures the call. None of it tells you what to do
differently on the next one.

Recording captured every word. It didn't capture the decision — the
moment the deal actually turned, which a transcript-scored solution
was never built to find. [Sales call transcript analysis
metrics](/blog/sales-call-transcript-analysis-metrics) covers why the
metrics and the moment are usually two different things.
