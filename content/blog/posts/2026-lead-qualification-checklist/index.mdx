Most lead qualification checklists are a points system.

Six or seven boxes, check what applies, move on. Nothing on the list
can fail a lead by itself.

Which means a rep with a number to protect can check every box
generously and never technically lie once.

## The list was never the problem

A checklist with six boxes and no gate isn't really filtering
anything.

It's recording what a rep was willing to claim, one small overstatement
at a time. "They mentioned budget" becomes budget confirmed. ["Someone
with that title was on the
call"](/blog/sales-lead-evaluation-framework) becomes decision-maker
engaged.

Add every box up and the lead clears the list — not because it's
strong, but because nothing on the list was ever allowed to say no.

## What a real checklist needs

One box that isn't a point. A gate.

Five items below are judgment calls, and reasonable reps will score
them differently. That's fine — judgment is supposed to vary.

The sixth one isn't a judgment call. It's a fact, checkable in one
sentence, and if the answer is no, nothing else on the list matters
yet.

## The six-item checklist

**1. Pain with a paper trail.** A budget line, a doc, a prior attempt
— something showing the org was already working the problem before
you showed up.

**2. One person who can say yes.** Not a title. A person who doesn't
have to check with someone else first. **This one is the gate** — if
the honest answer is "they'd have to ask," stop here. Everything below
is premature.

**3. A named cost of doing nothing.** Not "it would be nice." A number
or a consequence they can say out loud without you supplying it.

**4. Visible effort from the champion.** Did they set up the meeting
themselves, loop someone in unprompted, send you something you didn't
ask for? Effort spent before you asked for anything is hard to fake.

**5. A deadline that isn't yours.** A renewal, a compliance date, a
budget resetting next quarter — some clock the buyer doesn't control
either.

**6. The money-of-your-own test.** [Would you actually bet on this
deal](/blog/how-to-assess-a-deal-honestly), not just forecast it.

## Why item two has to be the gate

Item two is the only one on the list that's purely factual, not a read
on tone or intent.

Either a real spender said yes is possible without checking with
someone else, or they didn't. There's no generous way to round that
answer.

Make it the gate and a rep can't check five boxes generously and skip
past the one that actually mattered. [The same principle a longer
qualification framework runs on](/blog/meddic-questions) — the honest
version of a question has to cost something to answer.

## Run it before the lead has a number attached

The checklist only works in the window before a lead becomes a
pipeline entry — before it has a stage, a forecast category, or a
reason for you to want it to look better than it is.

[That window closes fast](/blog/b2b-lead-qualification). Once a lead
is logged, every later look at it happens through the lens of
protecting the number it already carries.
