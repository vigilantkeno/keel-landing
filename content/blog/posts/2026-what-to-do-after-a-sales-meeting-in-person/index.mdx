You walk out of the room. Elevator, stairwell, a walk to wherever
you're headed next — whatever's coming starts in about ninety
seconds.

Most advice about that moment assumes a car. Get in, shut the door,
talk it through before you pull out of the lot.

Fine advice, if you drove. Useless if you flew in for the visit,
walked between two offices downtown, or a rideshare is already
waiting at the curb.

## The car gets credit for the wrong thing

[We've written before](/blog/drive-home-from-sales-meeting) about
why the drive home works so well as a debrief. It's private. It's
unscheduled. Nobody's waiting on you yet.

None of that requires a windshield.

A stairwell works. A lobby chair works. The few minutes before your
next call starts on your calendar work, if you actually take them.

The car isn't the mechanism. It's just the most common place reps
happen to land five unscheduled minutes alone.

## What actually breaks the window

The read on a meeting is sharp for a few minutes and gone within the
hour. That decay is the same whether you're behind a wheel or
standing on a sidewalk.

What kills it fastest isn't a desk. It's your phone.

Check a text on your way out, and your attention resets onto
whatever that text says. By the time you're free again, the meeting
is already a memory of a memory.

The phone doesn't have to be a CRM field to do the damage. It just
has to come first.

## The five-minute sequence, wherever you are

Three things, in order, before you touch your phone for anything
else.

What changed. Not what was discussed — what's different now than
before you walked in.

Who said the thing that mattered. One line, not a transcript.

What you'd do differently next time. Even one sentence.

That's the whole sequence. Saying it takes less time than reading
this paragraph did.

## Field reps don't get a drive home — they get worse

Site visits, trade show floors, back-to-back office walk-ins: the
reps with the least predictable schedule are exactly the ones this
advice usually skips.

No car. No ten quiet minutes built into the day. Just the next thing,
immediately.

That's exactly who needs the sequence most. There's no commute
forcing the pause — you take it on purpose, or it doesn't happen.
