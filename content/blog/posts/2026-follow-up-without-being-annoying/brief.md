## Why this keyword, with evidence

Google autocomplete was used to validate demand before selection. Phrases that
*sound* like the market returned **zero** suggestions and were rejected:
`sales call debrief`, `what to do after a sales call`, `sales discovery call
notes`, `how to take notes during a sales call`, `champion went quiet`,
`prospect went silent`.

`how to follow up without being annoying` returned ten variants including
`how to follow up with a prospect without being annoying` — real, deep demand
with a prospect-specific cut available.

It was chosen over the higher-volume alternatives deliberately:

- **Template queries** (`sales call notes template`, `sales call log template
  excel`) have more volume but the intent is "give me a free Excel file."
  Wrong visitor for a product that is not a template.
- **Head terms** (`sales follow up email after no response`) are held by
  HubSpot, Close and Gong. getkeel.io has no backlink authority yet; that is a
  6–18 month fight, not a first post.

This query is winnable because the honest answer is differentiated, and
because answer engines reward the clearest specific answer rather than domain
authority.

## SERP notes

Dominated by template roundups and cadence advice — "follow up 5–7 times",
"use these 12 templates". Every top result treats *annoying* as a frequency
problem. None treat it as an information problem. That gap is the angle.

## Answer-first lead (first ~100 words)

Following up stops being annoying when the message carries something the
prospect did not already have. "Just checking in" is irritating because it is
empty, not because it is frequent. The fix is upstream of the email: capture
what actually happened in the last conversation, so the next message can
reference something real.
