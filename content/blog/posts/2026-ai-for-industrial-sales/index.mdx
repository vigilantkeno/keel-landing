A plant manager walks you the length of the floor, past three lines
you're not selling into, to the one that actually matters. Along the
way he mentions the maintenance window that controls your whole
timeline.

You won't write that down standing next to a running line. By the
time you're back in your truck, you're trying to remember which line
he meant.

## The assumption everyone starts from

Ask why industrial sales gets left out of the AI conversation and
you'll hear the same list. No desk. Spotty signal.

Hands full half the day. PPE instead of a laptop bag.

All true. All used as reasons the category doesn't apply here.

That reasoning has it backward.

## What the assumption actually gets wrong

Office sales AI assumes a rep who's already at a keyboard — someone
typing CRM notes between video calls, for whom a new tool is one
more tab.

An industrial rep was never that person. No keyboard habit to
compete with. No assumption that notes happen at a desk.

That's not a disadvantage. It's a rep whose day was never built
around typing in the first place — which makes a voice-first tool a
better fit here than in most offices, not a worse one.

## Where the connectivity problem actually lives

Most industrial sites have working cell coverage even where wifi is
weak or restricted. A phone call gets through in places a browser
tab won't load.

The tools that fail on a plant floor are the ones built around a
website — a dashboard, a form, a login screen. Not the ones built
around a call.

So "no signal" is usually a diagnosis of the wrong tool, not of the
environment.

## What actually gets lost on a facility visit

The maintenance window that sets the real timeline, mentioned once,
in passing.

The spec detail that ruled out the last vendor — usually said by
someone who wasn't in the room for the sales pitch.

The name of whoever actually signs off, which is almost never the
person giving the tour.

None of it survives as more than a vague impression by the time
you're back at your truck. All of it is the kind of detail a CRM
field was never built to catch.

## Why the drive back is the actual capture window

[The same problem shows up after any field visit](/blog/ai-for-in-person-sales-conversations)
that never had a recorder in the room to begin with — there's
nowhere for the detail to have gone except into your memory, and
memory fades fast on a drive.

Say it out loud before the next site, before the next call, before
the specifics blur into "the visit went well." That's the whole
method. It doesn't need wifi.

## Built for the floor, not the office

An AI for industrial sales that only works from a desk was never
actually built for industrial sales — it was built for an office rep
and pointed at a harder environment. [The same case applies anywhere
a deal happens somewhere a recorder never could](/blog/what-call-recording-cant-capture):
the tool has to meet the rep where the work already happens, not the
other way around.

That means a phone call, not a login. Available from the truck, the
loading dock, or the plant floor, the same way a phone call already
is.
