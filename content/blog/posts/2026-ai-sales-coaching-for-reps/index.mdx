Search "AI sales coaching for reps" and two entirely different products
come back wearing the same name.

Same screenshots. Same words — personalized, real-time, actionable.

Open the product itself and the two piles stop looking alike at all.

## What the first pile actually is

Most tools calling themselves AI sales coaching for reps are a scoring
layer bolted onto a review process that already existed.

[Gong scorecards](/blog/gong-scorecards) are the clearest version of
this — a rubric, applied by a model instead of a person, still routed to
whoever runs the pipeline review.

The AI part is real. The audience never changed.

You get a score. Your manager sees the same score, usually first.

## What the second pile actually is

The other product sold under the same name works differently. It talks
to the rep, close to the moment, with nothing routed upstream.

[Rep-first AI](/blog/rep-first-ai-sales) is the general version of that
idea — the read on a call belongs to the person who was on it, not to
whoever reviews it afterward.

That's the distinction "AI sales coaching for reps" should actually
draw. Most of the market just borrowed the phrase for the other pile,
because it tests better than "scorecard."

## The one question that sorts them

Ask any tool marketed this way a single thing: who sees the output
first, and can anyone else see it at all?

If the honest answer involves a login your manager also has, or a
review queue somebody else opens — that's automated grading wearing a
coaching label.

If the honest answer is "just you," that's the second pile. It's rarer
than the marketing suggests.

| What to check | Automated scorecard | Rep-first coaching |
|---|---|---|
| Who reads it first | The manager's review queue | The rep, alone |
| When it arrives | Weekly digest or review meeting | Minutes after the call |
| What it optimizes for | Conformity to a rubric | The rep's own judgment |

## Why the label keeps getting borrowed

"Coach" sells better than "scorer." Every vendor building the first
pile knows it.

Sharpening judgment isn't the same project as monitoring activity —
[the anxiety a scored call produces](/blog/gong-anxiety) is what
happens when a tool built to grade gets marketed as one built to help.
Reps think more honestly about a deal when nobody's reading the
transcript for a rubric.

That's the actual case for building the second kind instead of
relabeling the first.
