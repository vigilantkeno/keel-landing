Priya types "Gong criticism" into her phone from the parking garage,
headlights off, engine still running.

She isn't shopping for a competitor. She just watched her manager
scroll past three calls she was proud of to get to the one that
scored a 54.

The search results read like four different complaints.

They're not.

## What "Gong criticism" actually turns up

Search it yourself and a pattern repeats. Reps calling it invasive.
Reps calling the scorecards unfair. Reps describing something close
to dread before a monitored call.

A smaller thread about consent, and what's legal to record in states
that require it.

Four complaints. Four different threads, four different people typing
them at eleven at night, each one convinced their gripe is the odd one
out.

## One design choice, four complaints

Gong makes a recording of the call. Gong scores it. Gong shows both
to someone who wasn't on it.

[We've written about what that scoring actually measures](/blog/gong-scorecards)
— a call's resemblance to a rubric, not its effect on the deal.
[We've written about what waiting on the recording does to the rep who made it](/blog/gong-anxiety)
— the self-editing, the dread, the honest thing said in a stairwell
instead of on the record.

Both posts are really about the same decision. Recording a
conversation and grading it later turns selling into an audition.

Everything downstream of that — the anxiety, the scorecard
resentment, the "invasive" label, the legal exposure in states that
require consent — is a symptom. Not four bugs. One design choice,
said four ways.

## Why Gong can't fix this with a settings toggle

A softer rubric still grades the call. A private scorecard still means
someone graded it. Neither removes the thing reps are actually
reacting to: being recorded, then judged, by someone who wasn't there.

That's a structural fact about the product, not a rough edge a future
release smooths out.

Tools built to improve a rep's own judgment work differently than
tools built to monitor whether the rep is performing. Gong is
firmly the second kind. No update changes which one it is.

[Sales AI built for the rep instead of the manager starts from the
opposite premise](/blog/ai-sales-tool-no-manager-reporting) — nothing
recorded, nothing routed upward, nothing sitting in a system waiting
to be scored. That isn't a feature Gong could ship. It's the opposite
of what the product is for.

## None of this means Gong is bad software

It means Gong is built to answer to a manager, and reps searching
"Gong criticism" already feel that difference in their gut, even
before they can name it this plainly.

A conversation-intelligence platform aimed at leadership will always
read as invasive to the person on the call. That's not a flaw in the
execution. It's what the product is for, working correctly, from the
one seat it wasn't built to serve.

[Reps who raise privacy concerns aren't hiding anything](/blog/sales-rep-privacy-concerns)
— they're usually the ones being most honest about what a monitored
call costs them.

## The part of the complaint that's actually about the law

Not every "Gong criticism" search is about how a call feels. Some of
it is about whether recording it was even legal in the first place.

[Two-party consent states turn that from a discomfort into an
actual exposure](/blog/two-party-consent-states-sales-calls) — a
real compliance question sitting underneath what otherwise sounds
like a mood complaint.
