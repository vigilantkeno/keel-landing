"The pipeline's just slow right now" is the explanation every rep
reaches for first.

It's rarely the whole story. Almost always, one specific stage is
stuck — and everything before and after it is moving at a normal pace.

## The average hides where the drag actually is

A pipeline's overall cycle time is one number, averaged across every
deal in every stage.

That average can't tell you anything useful, because a slowdown
concentrated in a single stage and a genuine slowdown spread evenly
across all of them produce the exact same overall number.

Only one of those is a real crisis. The other is one stage with a
specific, fixable problem.

## Where the actual bottleneck usually sits

Break cycle time out by stage instead of looking at it in aggregate,
and one stage almost always stands out — both in average time and in
spread.

- **Right after the proposal goes out.** Some deals close in days.
  Others sit for months waiting on a decision nobody's forcing.
- **Security or legal review.** A stage that runs on someone else's
  calendar, not the rep's or the buyer's.
- **The gap after a strong first call.** Interest was real. A second
  meeting never got booked.

Each of those has a different fix. None of them get fixed by treating
the whole pipeline as uniformly slow.

## Why "slow" and "stalled" get treated as the same problem

They aren't.

A slow deal is still moving. It's just on a longer clock than the
average — a longer procurement cycle, a bigger buying committee, a
budget that doesn't open until next quarter.

[A stalled deal has actually stopped](/blog/how-to-wake-up-a-deal).
Calling it "slow" is usually the more comfortable word for a deal
that's quietly dead, because dead requires marking it closed-lost and
slow doesn't require anything at all.

## What actually separates the two

Ask one question, one deal at a time: has anything specific happened
since the last touch, or has time just passed?

A slow deal has an answer — a review board meets Thursday, procurement
confirmed budget, the champion is waiting on a signature from someone
else. [A stalled one usually doesn't](/blog/sales-pipeline-health),
and the silence where the answer should be is the actual diagnosis.

That answer lives with the rep who took the last call, not in the
stage-duration report. The report can only tell you where to go ask.
